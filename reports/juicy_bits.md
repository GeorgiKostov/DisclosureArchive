## Evidence Tiers

1. Strongest primary provenance: NASA transcripts/debriefings, State Department cables,
   FBI 302-style interview records, DOW mission reports, DVIDS-hosted videos.
2. Strongest case texture: multiple trained observers, specific times/places, sensor
   context, contemporaneous reporting, or repeated observations.
3. Weakest for interpretation: still images without mission reports, scanned legacy files
   without OCR, artist renderings, and narrative descriptions that explicitly warn they
   are not analytical conclusions.

## NASA And Moon Material

### Apollo 12, 1969

Local PDF:
`/Users/georgikostov/Desktop/ufo_war_release/documents/nasa-uap-d1-apollo-12-transcript-1969.pdf`

What matters:

- The release highlights a one-hour period on mission day 5 and a two-minute period
  on mission day 6.
- Alan Bean described light particles/flashes seen through the AOT, moving away into
  space; the description says he characterized some as "escaping the Moon."
- Pete Conrad later discussed small debris outside the lunar module and the tracking
  light context, which gives this a plausible spacecraft/debris frame.

Best read:

This is real NASA transcript material with astronaut observations. It is interesting
because it is contemporaneous lunar-mission testimony, but the second episode has a
clear debris/tracking-light context.

### Apollo 17, 1972

Local PDF:
`/Users/georgikostov/Desktop/ufo_war_release/documents/nasa-uap-d2-apollo-17-transcript-1972.pdf`

What matters:

- Ronald Evans and Jack Schmitt described bright, tumbling fragments near the spacecraft.
- The crew discussed ice, paint, or S-IVB fragments as possible explanations.
- Gene Cernan later described flashing/rotating objects that he believed were physical
  and distant, not merely nearby particles.
- Schmitt reported a brief flash on the lunar surface north of Grimaldi and suggested
  checking seismometers.

Best read:

This is one of the richest NASA records in the release. It contains both anomalous-feeling
observations and on-the-record astronaut speculation about mundane spacecraft debris.
The lunar-surface flash near Grimaldi is the cleanest "moon weirdness" item.

## Military / DOW Material

### Western US Event, 2023

Local PDF:
`/Users/georgikostov/Desktop/ufo_war_release/documents/western_us_event_slides_5.08.2026.pdf`

What matters:

- Seven federal employees reported several events over two days.
- Three teams independently reported orange orbs emitting smaller red orbs.
- Two federal law enforcement agents reported a large glowing orange orb at dusk.
- AARO later estimated the fiery orb at roughly 12-18 meters in diameter and about
  1,050 meters from observers.
- Pre-dawn agents reported a dark/kite-like object moving laterally off-road without
  obvious resistance, then a later possibly transparent kite-shaped object.
- The release explicitly says there is no direct technical data, but calls the report
  among AARO's more compelling holdings due to credibility/context/anomalous features.

Best read:

This is probably the most narratively compelling case in the release. Its weakness is
the lack of direct sensor package; its strength is trained federal observers, repeated
events, and AARO's own language about significance.

### USPER Statement, Late 2025

Local PDF:
`/Users/georgikostov/Desktop/ufo_war_release/documents/usper-statement-redacted.pdf`

What matters:

- FBI interview with a senior U.S. intelligence official.
- Helicopter/search teams were responding to prior orb/light reports at a military facility.
- LP/OP personnel using FLIR/NVG reported a super-hot orb near ground level.
- The report says it broke into two objects, moved too fast for helicopter pursuit,
  and something appeared to emerge from the two objects under NVG.
- Later sightings involved repeated horizontal orb formations flaring up/down and
  swarms of lights over roughly 30 minutes.

Best read:

This is one of the strongest "modern weird" records: senior official, helicopter,
FLIR/NVG/naked-eye context, multiple observers. It is still witness/report text, not a
final analytical determination.

## State Department Cables

### Kazakhstan, 1994

Local PDF:
`/Users/georgikostov/Desktop/ufo_war_release/documents/dos-uap-d2-cable-2-kazakhstan-january-1994.pdf`

What matters:

- Tajik Air chief pilot and American colleagues reported an object while flying a 747SP
  at 41,000 feet over Kazakhstan.
- Observed for about 40 minutes.
- Reported circles, corkscrews, 90-degree turns, high speed, and high-G maneuvering.
- Crew said contrails seemed around 100,000 feet.
- Cable itself ends with no official opinion.

Best read:

Possibly the best old-school aviation report in the release. It is not a military sensor
case, but the witness profile and cable form make it stronger than folklore.

### Papua New Guinea, 1985

Local PDF:
`/Users/georgikostov/Desktop/ufo_war_release/documents/dos-uap-d1-cable-1-papua-new-guinea-january-1985.pdf`

What matters:

- PNG intelligence asked the U.S. Embassy about high-altitude/high-speed overflights.
- Reports included fast objects, lights, contrails, and noise.
- An Air Niugini pilot's radar report gave the NIO some confidence.
- Embassy found no known B-52 or U.S. aircraft overflight in PNG airspace that night
  and requested confirmation/context.

Best read:

Good diplomatic record of a regional airspace mystery. The cable itself calls the
information sketchy, so treat it as a real inquiry rather than a resolved UAP case.
