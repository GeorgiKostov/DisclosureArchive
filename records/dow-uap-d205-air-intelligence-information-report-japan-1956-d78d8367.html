DOW-UAP-D205, Air Intelligence Information Report, Japan, 1956

Department of War · 12/17/56 · Japan · Document

DOW-UAP-D205, Air Intelligence Information Report, Japan, 1956 is indexed as a Department of War record with incident date 12/17/56 and location Japan. This document is an Air Intelligence Information Report (AIIR), a standardized U.S. Air Force reporting form used to record raw information of potential intelligence value for later analysis. The form served as a primary vehicle for U.S. Air Force field intelligence collection throughout the early Cold War, until defense-wide reporting standards were established in the early 1960s. This AIIR records a December 17, 1956, incident in the vicinity of Ube, Japan, in which two U.S. Air Force F-86D pilots were conducting practice intercepts under ground-controlled radar direction when one pilot reported detecting a second radar target, separate from the other F-86D, at a range of 20 nautical miles. The report states that the unidentified return appeared larger than the return from the other aircraft and remained visible on the interceptor s radar scope for approximately seven to eight minutes. It further states that the pilot later obtained intermittent visual contact for approximately three to four minutes after closing to within about five nautical miles, describing the object as best resembling the upper half of a sphere. The report also notes possible electronic countermeasures interference on the F-86D radar scope and estimates the object s rate of departure at 1,500 to 1,800 knots (1,726 2,071 mph) after the radar return moved rapidly off the top of the scope, while noting that the figure exceeded what the radar set could directly indicate for a negative rate of closure. The report further states that neither ground-based radar sites nor the second pilot acquired the object on radar. All descriptive and estimative language contained in this report reflects the reporter s subjective interpretation at the time of the event. Such characterizations should not be interpreted as a conclusive indication of the presence or absence of any intrinsic object features or performance characteristics.
