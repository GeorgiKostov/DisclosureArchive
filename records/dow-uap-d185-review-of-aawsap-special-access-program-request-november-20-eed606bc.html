DOW-UAP-D185, Review of AAWSAP Special Access Program Request, November 2009

Department of War · 11/13/09 · Washington, D.C. · Document

DOW-UAP-D185, Review of AAWSAP Special Access Program Request, November 2009 is indexed as a Department of War record with incident date 11/13/09 and location Washington, D.C.. This document is an administrative or programmatic record related to the Advanced Aerospace Weapon System Applications Program (AAWSAP), a Defense Intelligence Agency (DIA)-administered program active from 2008 to 2012. AAWSAP s official scope of work identified 12 technical research areas relating to potential aerospace threats over a time horizon of more than 40 years. As an administrative record, the file documents how AAWSAP was scoped, organized, tasked, funded, or described at a particular point in time. This November 2009 DIA information memorandum evaluates Senator Harry Reid s June 2009 request (DOW-UAP-D183) to place portions of the AAWSAP contract, described in his letter under the alternate name Advanced Aerospace Threat and Identification Program (AAITP), into a restricted Special Access Program (SAP). The memorandum concludes that DIA could not find adequate justification for such a step, stating that all first-year program documents had been unclassified, that the contractor had not established a secure facility during that period, and that DIA was unaware of any delivered report containing information sensitive enough to warrant additional compartmentation. It further argues that the available material did not meet the threshold for original classification, in part because much of it consisted of publicly available scientific or technical information, and it characterizes Reid s claims of highly sensitive findings as more plausibly referring to future phases of the effort than to the reports already delivered. In effect, the document is a formal internal finding that Senator Reid s SAP request was not warranted, grounded in DIA s view that the program s first-year outputs did not justify heightened protection beyond normal classification procedures.
